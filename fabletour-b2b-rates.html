Day-by-day program

Day 1Baghdad · Babylon · Karbala

Babylon & Karbala

Ancient Mesopotamian grandeur meets spiritual devotion.

Arrival at Baghdad International Airport followed by a seamless transfer to your hotel for rest. The journey begins with Babylon, the legendary capital of Mesopotamian civilization. You will walk the Processional Way, marvel at the foundations of the Ishtar Gate, and explore ruins that represent the cradle of human law, science, and urban life, including the legacy of the Code of Hammurabi. We then journey to Borsippa to see the remains of the great Ziggurat of Nabu, the god of wisdom, a site vital to the intellectual history of the region. A stop at the Shrine of Prophet Job (Ayub) follows, a place of peace and profound interfaith significance. As the day transitions to evening, we move to the sacred city of Karbala. Here, you will visit the Imam Husayn and Al-Abbas shrines. These sites are not merely architectural masterpieces with their glistening domes and intricate tile work; they are powerful, living monuments to justice, sacrifice, and the enduring human spirit. The atmosphere is one of profound tranquility and deep cultural immersion, providing a stark and beautiful contrast to the ancient power of Babylon explored earlier in the day. Overnight in Karbala provides time to reflect on this powerful start to your journey.

Overnight Karbala — The Baron Hotel Karbala (5★)
Day 2Kufa · Najaf

Najaf & Kufa

Deep-dive into the theological history of Islamic civilization.

This day is dedicated to the intellectual and spiritual pillars of Islamic history in Iraq. We depart for Najaf and Kufa. In Kufa, we visit the Great Mosque of Kufa, one of the oldest in the world, which served as a central hub for early Islamic governance. You will see the House of Imam Ali, offering a rare, personal glimpse into the life of one of history's most revered figures. We also visit the Shrine of Maytham al-Tammar, a site of great historical importance. A somber yet fascinating visit follows at Wadi Al-Salam, the largest cemetery on Earth. Walking through this 'Valley of Peace' reveals the history of countless generations, stretching back over a millennium and housing the graves of prophets, kings, and scholars. We conclude the experience in Najaf at the Shrine of Imam Ali. The massive gold dome and the sheer scale of the shrine, combined with the thousands of pilgrims performing their devotions, offer an unparalleled look at the depth of faith in the region. This day provides a comprehensive understanding of how the theological currents here have shaped Islamic governance, philosophy, and history for centuries, grounding you in the profound legacy of Najaf as a center of global scholarship and pilgrimage.

Overnight Najaf — Granada, Do Najaf or Naseem Al-Najaf Hotel (5★)
Day 3Uruk · Basra

Uruk & Basra by Sunset

From the birth of writing to the Venice of the East.

We head south to the ancient city of Uruk, famous as the world's first true city-state and the birthplace of the written word. It is here that the Epic of Gilgamesh was brought to life, and standing amidst these ruins connects you to the very origin of recorded human history. We then continue to Basra, long known as the 'Venice of the East'. As the light softens, we take in the sunset over the Shatt al-Arab, where the Tigris and Euphrates flow together towards the Gulf — one of the most beautiful moments of the journey. In the evening we explore Old Basra, walking through streets lined with traditional houses and their intricate Shanasheel wooden balconies, which tell the story of the city's golden age as a hub of trade between the Gulf, India and beyond. We end the day in the lively Grand Bazaar of Basra, among spices, dates, fabrics and the everyday life of this historic port city. Overnight in Basra.

Overnight Basra — Mövenpick Zinat Al-Hayat or Corniche Hotel (5★)
Day 4Qurna · The Marshes · Ur → Baghdad

Adam's Tree, the Marshes & Ur

Living traditions and the monuments of Sumer.

We leave Basra for Qurna, where the Tigris and Euphrates meet, to visit the legendary Adam's Tree, a symbolic site deeply embedded in local tradition and oral history. The landscape then opens onto the Mesopotamian Marshes, a UNESCO-listed ecological sanctuary. You will experience a way of life that has thrived here for thousands of years, gliding along the waterways by traditional boat and seeing the iconic 'Mudhif' reed guest houses, a truly sustainable architectural tradition. We continue to the Ziggurat of Ur, a monumental Sumerian masterpiece of engineering and architectural sophistication, whose mud-brick terraces have endured for over four thousand years as a testament to the skill of the ancient Mesopotamians. In the afternoon we drive north, watching the landscapes of Iraq change on the road to the capital, and arrive in Baghdad in the evening.

Overnight Baghdad — Do Baghdad, Alabasly Suites or Bilitum Hotel (5★)
Day 5Samarra · Mosul

Samarra & Northern Journey

The architectural ambition of the Abbasid era.

We head north to the city of Samarra, which, for a brief period in the 9th century, served as the capital of the Abbasid Caliphate. The sheer scale of this city and the ambition of its builders remain evident in the ruins today. The absolute highlight is the Malwiya Minaret of the Great Mosque of Samarra. This massive, spiraling, helical structure is an architectural icon of the Islamic world, representing the power and innovation of the Caliphate at its absolute peak. Climbing the spiral path provides a panoramic view of the ancient city and the surrounding desert, offering a sense of the immense scale of the urban planning that existed here over 1,000 years ago. We continue to the Al-Askari Shrine, the resting place of Imam Ali al-Hadi and Imam Hasan al-Askari. The shrine is a work of immense artistic detail, famous for its brilliant gold-leaf dome that glows in the desert light. After exploring the profound architectural and religious heritage of Samarra, we continue our journey further north toward the vibrant city of Mosul. This leg of the trip illustrates the geographical breadth of Iraqi history, moving from the political heart of the Abbasid Caliphate into the northern reaches of the country. Overnight in Mosul, with a chance to experience the unique culinary and cultural traditions of the north.

Overnight Mosul — Ramada Plaza by Wyndham Mosul Corniche (5★)
Day 6Old Mosul · Nimrud · Hatra

Mosul & Ancient Empires

Resilience, history, and the ruins of Hatra.

The day begins with a tour of Old Mosul. Despite the challenges of recent years, the city's spirit is alive and resilient. You will witness the ongoing efforts to restore the historic heart of the city, including its famous mosques and traditional markets. From Mosul, we set out to visit Nimrud, an ancient Assyrian capital. Exploring the remnants here provides a stark look at the massive architectural projects and artistic power of the Assyrian kings who once dominated the region. The day's climax is the visit to Hatra, a UNESCO World Heritage site and arguably one of the most beautiful archaeological sites in the Middle East. Hatra was a fortified city that functioned as a vital trading hub and a defensive bastion on the borders of empires. The site is a masterclass in architectural fusion, blending Hellenistic, Roman, and Parthian motifs. The incredibly preserved temple structures and defensive walls stand hauntingly beautiful against the backdrop of the desert. After spending the day traveling through these layers of empires, you will have a deep sense of the historical importance of the northern region. We return to Baghdad in the evening, carrying with us the memory of the resilience and the rich historical legacy of Mosul and its surrounding empires.

Overnight Baghdad — Do Baghdad, Alabasly Suites or Bilitum Hotel (5★)
Day 7Baghdad → Departure

Baghdad Grand Finale

A synthesis of ancient and modern Iraq.

Our final day is a tribute to Baghdad, the legendary 'City of Peace'. We start by visiting the magnificent shrines of Imam Musa al-Kadhim and Imam Muhammad al-Jawad, which stand as the spiritual heartbeat of the capital. The intricate artistry and the devotion of the pilgrims create an atmosphere of deep reflection. We then transition into an Old Baghdad walking tour. This includes a stroll through historic tea houses, lively markets, and the world-famous Al-Mutanabbi Street, which has been the center of the Arab literary world for centuries. You will have time to browse through the books, interact with local writers, and enjoy a traditional tea by the banks of the Tigris. In the afternoon, we travel to the monumental Aqar Quf Ziggurat. This structure, dating back to the Kassite period, is remarkably well-preserved and serves as a final, powerful reminder of the sophisticated engineering and architectural traditions of ancient Mesopotamia. Baghdad offers the perfect synthesis of your trip, blending the ancient history of the region with the bustling, warm, and resilient energy of a modern capital. This final day is a time to reflect on your journey through the 'Land of Civilizations' and celebrate the incredible hospitality and deep heritage of the Iraqi people before your departure.

Day-by-day program

Day 1Basra

Basra, Gateway of the South

Where the Tigris and Euphrates flow together to the Gulf.

Arrival at Basra International Airport, where our team meets the group and transfers you to the hotel. After check-in and lunch, the program opens in Iraq's great port city. We visit the Armenian Church of the Holy Virgin Mary, a quiet testimony to Basra's long history as a meeting place of communities, and continue to the Basra Museum, set in a former palace on the river, whose galleries trace the south from Sumer and Babylon to the Islamic era. In the evening we walk through the city centre and along the Shatt al-Arab, where the Tigris and Euphrates join on their way to the Gulf, and watch the sunset over the palm groves.

Overnight Basra — Mövenpick Zinat Al-Hayat (5★)
Day 2Marshes · Ur · Najaf

The Marshes & the Ziggurat of Ur

Living traditions and the monuments of Sumer.

An early start takes us to the Mesopotamian Marshes, a UNESCO-listed landscape of reed islands, water buffalo and still waterways where the Marsh Arabs have lived for thousands of years, building their arched mudhif guest houses entirely from reeds. We continue to Ur, the Sumerian city of the 3rd millennium BC, and climb the Great Ziggurat, whose mud-brick terraces have stood for over four thousand years. In the afternoon we drive north to the holy city of Najaf.

Overnight Najaf — Do Najaf Hotel
Day 3Najaf · Kufa · Karbala

Najaf, Kufa & Karbala

A day in the great centres of Islamic scholarship and pilgrimage.

In Najaf we visit the Shrine of Imam Ali, its golden dome surrounded by courtyards filled with pilgrims from across the world. Nearby lies Wadi al-Salam, the 'Valley of Peace', one of the largest cemeteries on Earth, stretching back more than a thousand years. In Kufa we see the Great Mosque of Kufa, one of the oldest mosques in the world and a seat of early Islamic government. We then drive to Karbala (80 km) to visit the shrines of Imam Hussein and Al-Abbas, facing each other across a wide esplanade, with their glittering domes and fine tile work.

Overnight Karbala — The Baron Hotel Karbala (5★)
Day 4Babylon · Borsippa · Baghdad

Babylon & Borsippa

The capital of Hammurabi and Nebuchadnezzar.

A short drive (55 km) brings us to Babylon, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most famous cities of the ancient world. We walk the Processional Way, see the reconstructed Ishtar Gate and the foundations of Nebuchadnezzar's palaces, and recall the legacy of the Code of Hammurabi. At Borsippa we stand before the ruined Ziggurat of Nabu, god of wisdom, and then stop at the Shrine of Prophet Ayyub (Job). In the afternoon we continue to Baghdad (115 km) and end the day with an evening walk in the capital.

Overnight Baghdad — Alabasly Suites (5★)
Day 5Baghdad · Aqar Quf

Baghdad, City of Peace

The capital of the Abbasid Caliphate, old and new.

A full day in the capital. We walk through the historic centre of Old Baghdad, along Al-Rashid Street to Al-Mutanabbi Street, the city's book market, and the Ottoman Qishla clock tower by the Tigris. We visit the Martyrs' Monument, with its split turquoise dome, and the golden Al-Kadhimiya Mosque, one of the most beautiful shrines in Iraq. In the afternoon we drive to the edge of the city to the Ziggurat of Aqar Quf, built by the Kassites about 3,400 years ago and still rising high above the plain. Free time in the evening.

Overnight Baghdad — Alabasly Suites (5★)
Day 6Samarra · Hatra · Mosul

Samarra & Hatra

The Abbasid capital and the desert city of the Parthians.

An early departure north to Samarra (130 km), capital of the Abbasid Caliphate in the 9th century and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its icon is the Malwiya, the 52-metre spiral minaret of the Great Mosque, with wide views over the ancient city from its ramp. We also see the golden dome of the Al-Askari Shrine. We continue to Hatra, a fortified caravan city of the Parthian era and a UNESCO site, whose temples blend Hellenistic, Roman and Eastern styles and stand in the open desert. In the evening we reach Mosul. This is the longest driving day of the program.

Overnight Mosul — Ramada Plaza by Wyndham Mosul Corniche
Day 7Old Mosul · Nimrud · Mar Mattai · Erbil

Mosul, Nimrud & Mar Mattai

Assyrian kings and a monastery on the mountain.

We begin in the Old City of Mosul on the banks of the Tigris, with its traditional markets and stone houses, and the Great Mosque of al-Nuri with its famous leaning minaret, Al-Hadba. We then drive to Nimrud (40 km), capital of the Assyrian king Ashurnasirpal II, whose palaces were once guarded by winged lamassu. Our next stop is Mar Mattai, a Syriac monastery founded in the 4th century and set high on Mount Alfaf, with sweeping views over the Nineveh Plain. In the late afternoon we continue to Erbil.

Overnight Erbil — Erbil International Hotel (5★)
Day 8Erbil

Erbil, the Ancient Citadel

One of the oldest continuously inhabited places on Earth.

A day in the capital of the Kurdistan Region. We walk through the historic centre and climb to the Erbil Citadel, a UNESCO World Heritage Site on a mound that has been inhabited for thousands of years. At its foot lies the Qaysari Bazaar, a covered market of spices, sweets, fabrics and local crafts. We visit the Erbil Civilization Museum and relax in Shanadar Park. The evening is free to enjoy the cafés and restaurants of the city.

Overnight Erbil — Erbil International Hotel (5★)
Day 9Erbil → Departure

Departure

Farewell to the Land Between the Rivers.

Breakfast at the hotel, check-out and transfer to Erbil International Airport for the flight home.
